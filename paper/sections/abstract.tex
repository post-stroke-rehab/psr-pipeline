Post-stroke finger-intent decoding from surface electromyography (sEMG) is a promising control pathway for rehabilitation wearables, but progress is often difficult to interpret because datasets, preprocessing, models, and deployment assumptions are reported in isolation. This paper presents the software portion of the \repo\ research project, whose long-term goal is to serve compact machine-learning models on Raspberry Pi 5-class hardware for a finger-assist rehabilitation system~\citep{psrrepo2026,physiomioData2026,raspberrypi52023}. The current software stack ingests raw \physiomio\ recordings, aligns gesture labels to five-finger multilabel targets, applies a fixed 20--450 Hz Butterworth filter, Symlet-4 wavelet denoising, and 200 ms windows with 50\% overlap, extracts twelve handcrafted descriptors per channel-window pair, and feeds a shared tensor representation into LSTM, graph neural network, and 1D CNN model families. Under this common pipeline, the three direct model families reveal complementary strengths rather than a single dominant baseline: the LSTM achieves the best exact-match accuracy among the original baselines (0.545), the GNN attains the highest baseline macro F1 (0.706) and macro AUPRC (0.776), and the legacy CNN trails both. Subsequent architectural refinement shifts the frontier toward compact convolutional models: the Optuna-tuned CNN-Large student reaches 0.593 exact-match accuracy, 0.798 finger accuracy, 0.714 macro F1, 0.881 macro AUROC, and 0.812 macro AUPRC on the impaired-arm test split, slightly exceeding the best committed ResNet teacher on most aggregate metrics while remaining within an embedded-oriented size envelope. Two improvement pathways are also now visible in the repository: healthy-to-impaired transfer learning clearly benefits the CNN branch but not the LSTM branch, and teacher-ensemble distillation is implemented as a speed-quality pathway even though a fully committed distilled-student evaluation bundle is not yet present. We therefore present this manuscript as an evidence-constrained research paper on a developing neurorehabilitation software stack rather than as a claim of clinical readiness or a cross-paper state-of-the-art result.
