\section{Conclusion}

This paper studied five-finger intent decoding from post-stroke HD-sEMG using a shared signal-processing and tensor-adaptation pipeline. Direct LSTM, CNN, and GNN baselines showed complementary strengths, with the LSTM leading exact-match accuracy and the GNN leading macro F1 and AUPRC. Subsequent CNN architecture search substantially improved the convolutional branch: CNN-Large reached the strongest offline aggregate performance, while CNN-Micro was selected for hardware deployment because it preserved most of that performance with 158K parameters and an estimated 154 KB INT8 footprint.

Healthy-to-impaired transfer learning further improved the CNN branch but produced mixed LSTM behavior, highlighting the architecture-specific nature of transfer in paretic sEMG decoding. In the context of prior sEMG and post-stroke rehabilitation studies, these results show that compact convolutional decoders can remain competitive with substantially larger ResNet teachers while operating on an impaired-arm, patient-level, multilabel finger-intent task. More broadly, the study frames post-stroke finger-intent decoding as an integrated design problem in which signal processing, model family, transfer strategy, and deployment constraints must be optimized together to support responsive Raspberry Pi-class rehabilitation hardware.
